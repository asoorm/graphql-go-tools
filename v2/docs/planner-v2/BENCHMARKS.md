## 3. Tree-vs-folded gap distribution (`TestTreeVsFoldedGapMeasured_AuditCorpus`)

Extends `search.TestTreeVsFoldedGapMeasured` (small synthetic hypergraphs, `FORMAL_SPEC.md` Section 8) to the
real audit corpus: for every case where `hypergraph.Build` -> `obligation.Build` -> `search.Search`
succeeds (independent of whether it plan-level PASSes/GAPs/SKIPs in `audit.Run` -- this measurement sits
one layer below lowering, so it also covers cases `audit.Run` SKIPs for the M1 argument-lowering gap),
records `gap = Sum tree-pi(covered goals) - folded C(K)` and asserts only the P1 direction
(`C(K) <= Sum tree-pi`) -- never folded optimality.

**200 audit cases measured** (of 202 transcribed; 2 skipped -- cases whose config/normalization/search
step errors before reaching a `search.Result`, a superset of `audit.Run`'s SKIP set since this bypasses
lowering entirely). The measured set grew sharply since the previous run (183 of 202, 19 skipped) --
the D7pp requires-resolution + type-cycle and D7ppp distributed-@key waves made 17 previously-skipped
cases searchable, and the same waves changed per-case candidate selection broadly (e.g.
abstract-types/case-03's tree Sum(pi) rose 20,076->36,148, making it the new max at 34,128;
simple-requires-provides/case-12's tree Sum(pi) dropped 32,272->18,150, pushing the old max out of the
top-5; requires-with-argument-conflict/case-01 dropped 21,037->9,005). The distribution shift at
that run (median 1,000->2,000, mean 2,750.4->3,179.5, zero-gap 39%->32%) was a mix of those
newly-measured cases and the candidate-selection changes -- not directly comparable case-for-case
with the earlier table. Re-earned at `b9bad396`: the distribution is essentially unchanged -- every
order statistic, the zero-gap count, and the top-5 table below are identical, and only the mean
moved (3,179.5->3,184.5; a handful of mid-distribution candidate selections shifted under the
encoding/narrowing waves).

| Statistic | Value |
|---|---:|
| min | 0 |
| p25 | 0 |
| median | 2,000 |
| p75 | 4,014 |
| p90 | 8,034 |
| **max** | **34,128** |
| **mean** | **3,184.5** |
| zero-gap cases (folding found no sharing) | 64 / 200 (32%) |

Largest-gap cases (folding saves the most vs. the naive per-goal tree sum):

| Case | Tree Sum(pi) | Folded C(K) | Gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| abstract-types/case-03 | 36,148 | 2,020 | 34,128 |
| child-type-mismatch/case-03 | 31,069 | 3,016 | 28,053 |
| override-with-requires/case-01 | 30,150 | 5,036 | 25,114 |
| complex-entity-call/case-01 | 27,126 | 3,028 | 24,098 |
| abstract-types/case-16 | 21,123 | 2,018 | 19,105 |

**Headline: folding never increases cost (0 P1 violations across 200 cases), and on this corpus it is
frequently a large, real reduction** -- the median case saves 2,000 (two fetch-weight units) and the
tail saves over 34,000, driven by cases with heavy shared sub-hyperpath reuse (repeated `@requires`
chains, abstract-type member fan-out). 32% of cases show zero gap -- a single dominant derivation path
with no exploitable sharing, which is itself useful signal: those are the cases where folding has
nothing to do and MERGE's co-location pass is the only remaining lever (see Section 2 above).

Implementation note for anyone extending this measurement: a goal's selected candidate may have been
chosen against a **masked** pi table under D10 path-consistency (multi-root operations -- vacuous for
the `search` package's own synthetic property-test instances, but exercised throughout the real audit
corpus). Comparing against the raw `res.Pi` instead of `res.PiFor(g)` produces spurious P1 "violations"
that are an artifact of comparing the folded cost of a masked derivation against the wrong (unmasked)
tree cost -- not a real defect. `gap_test.go` uses `PiFor`.

## 4. Complexity scaling (`BenchmarkSearchComplexityScaling`)

`search.Search` over generated acyclic hypergraphs (extending `property_test.go`'s deterministic
instance generator) at increasing `|E|`, 3 goals, guaranteed reachability. No assertion -- measurement
only, for the near-linear-scaling claim.

| Target \|E\| | Actual \|E\| | \|V\| | ns/op | ns/edge | B/op | allocs/op |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | 103 | 93 | 5,807 | 56.4 | 19,776 | 89 |
| 500 | 503 | 452 | 19,912 | 39.6 | 89,798 | 125 |
| 2,000 | 2,003 | 1,767 | 85,374 | 42.6 | 339,936 | 191 |
| 8,000 | 8,003 | 7,019 | 280,104 | 35.0 | 1,289,154 | 251 |

(Table of record measured at `b9bad396` -- the conformance-complete commit; benchstat medians,
n=10, `-benchtime=200ms`, quiesced machine, strictly serial.)

**Headline: ns/edge stays within 35-56 across two orders of magnitude of |E|, sub-linear again** --
80x more edges (100 -> 8,000) costs 48x more time (5,807ns -> 280,104ns), and allocs/op does not
grow with \|E\| (89 -> 251 across the same 80x -- container resizing inside the per-goal layered
traces, not a per-\|E\| term). B/op DOES grow roughly with \|V\| -- that is the chain-layered
consistent trace's per-goal state (see the historical chain below for what is recovered and what
remains); read the scaling story off `ns/edge`.

> **Historical chain (regression -> recovery -> cache -> this run; read before comparing against
> older snapshots).** The committed `f80e07e9` constant was 44.7-50.3 ns/edge with ~constant
> allocs (~50-150); the `6e1c40a5` recovery took E=8000 to 16.3-17.9 ns/edge. The class-C/D10
> wave **`4700b8c2`** (abstract-position member expansion + D10 chain-layered consistent trace)
> then regressed the kernel to **107-134 ns/edge with B/op 4-16x and allocs O(\|E\|)** -- bisected
> exactly at the `1daf64e1` re-measurement (+716% ns/op at E=8000 vs `4700b8c2~1`, p=0.008, n=5;
> CPU profile named `buildLayeredIndex`, an O(\|V\|+\|E\|) index that is a pure function of H but
> was rebuilt on EVERY `Search` call -- and on this synthetic family the chain-layered re-trace
> fires for essentially every goal). **`ba4a63d5` caches that index once per hypergraph**
> (`hypergraph.Memo`, atomic publish; plans byte-identical, all oracles green), recovering -62% to
> -78% ns/op and landing at 32.5-48.6 ns/edge with bounded allocs (89-251). **Re-earned at
> `b9bad396`:** B/op and allocs/op are byte-identical to `ba4a63d5` -- the waves since
> (subscriptions, defer, executed-encoding, reachability gaps) did not change the kernel's
> allocation profile at all (the window's single `search` commit is D11.12's operation-kind root
> scoping) -- while wall time reads +8-18% (E=100 +16.0%, E=500 +18.2% -- over the 15% material bar
> and flagged as such; E=2000 +9.7%, E=8000 +7.6%), consistent with the session's ambient shift
> (see the methodology block) plus at most a small real per-call branch cost.
>
> **Residual, still real:** at E=8000 the constant is ~2x the `6e1c40a5` recovery (35.0 vs 16.3
> ns/edge) and B/op is ~7.9x the `f80e07e9` committed value (162,936 -> 1,289,154). That is the
> chain-layered trace itself: each goal whose default walk is not chain-consistent (essentially
> every goal on this instance family -- an unrepresentatively high repair rate) runs a layered
> Dijkstra allocating O(\|V\|*(D+1)) per-trace state. That state is per-call by semantics (it
> depends on the goal's mask and settle table), not cacheable per-H; the remaining lever would be
> pooling the per-trace buffers across goals/Searches. Conformance-motivated (the corpus-complete
> result requires the chain-layered trace), and reported rather than hidden.

### 5.1 The synthetic supergraph

`generateScaleSupergraph` builds a RING of S entity types (`Entity0`, `Entity1`, ...) connected by a
`next: Entity(i+1 mod S)` reference field -- subgraph i owns `Entity_i` fully (id key, filler fields,
`next`/`profile`/`stats`/`meta`) and carries a bare reference STUB of `Entity_(i+1)` (just the key
field, so `next`'s output type resolves and the ring can be walked). On top of the ring, a
deterministic sprinkle (`i % 12`) adds: a second, independent single-field key (`@key(fields:"sku")`)
on ~1/12 of entities (multi-key), a nested/composite key (`@key(fields:"id region { code }")`,
entity_jump.go's shape) on ~1/12 (composite key), and an `@requires`-driven derived field on ~1/12
(requires). A shared, non-entity `Metadata` value type is declared identically in every subgraph
(the "shared value type" requirement). `FieldsPerEntity=300` filler scalar fields (each carrying a
short description doc-comment, approximating a real schema's per-field documentation overhead)
control the SDL byte footprint independently of the ring's structural complexity.

| S | Filler fields/entity | Aggregate SDL bytes | Composed types | \|V\| (H) | \|E\| (H) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 50 | 300 | 3,812,206 (3.81MB) | 153 | 15,944 | 16,031 |
| 100 | 300 | 7,627,352 (7.63MB) | 303 | 31,882 | 32,055 |
| 200 | 300 | 15,288,737 (**15.29MB**) | 603 | 63,771 | 64,124 |

(The SDL/type columns are unchanged from the previous run; \|V\|/\|E\| grew slightly at `1daf64e1`
-- e.g. S=200 63,754->63,771 / 64,056->64,124 -- because the D7ppp distributed-@key wave adds
key-gathering nodes/edges to `hypergraph.Build`'s output on the sprinkled multi-key entities.
Re-verified byte-identical at `b9bad396`: Section 5.5's build log still reads \|V\|=63,771 \|E\|=64,124 at
S=200 -- the subscription/defer/encoding/reachability waves did not change this topology's graph.)

S=200 lands almost exactly on the ~15MB target the M15 brief set, with 603 composed types (>500) and
64,124 hypergraph edges. `TestScaleSupergraphComposes` (`-race`) asserts `hypergraph.Build` succeeds
cleanly at all four sizes tested (S=2, 8, 30, 200) and that S=200 clears the 500-type bar.
`TestScaleSanityPlan` and `TestScaleMixedCapabilitiesPlan` (`-race`) confirm planv2 actually PLANS
operations on this topology end to end, including the sprinkled multi-key/composite-key/`@requires`
capabilities (not just inert SDL text) -- all pass.

`BenchmarkScaleHypergraphBuild`: dataSources generated once per S outside the timed loop; only the

subsequent `Plan` call against that supergraph -- a real gateway pays this once per schema change, not
per request.

| S | ns/op | B/op | allocs/op |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 50 | 18,827,334 (18.8ms) | 66,626,720 (66.6MB) | 130,719 |

| 200 | 84,503,333 (**84.5ms**) | 282,002,080 (**282.0MB**) | 522,340 |

**Headline: `hypergraph.Build` scales linearly in S on this topology** -- doubling S roughly doubles

v1 (`plan.NewPlanner`+`Plan`, fresh planner per op -- the same construction-inclusive convention Section 1's

v1's construction step does not do anything resembling planv2's eager `hypergraph.Build` compile
(Section 5.2's 17.8-75.2ms) -- it is us-scale regardless of S, allocating a near-constant ~29 objects. This
is not an apples-to-apples "who compiles faster" comparison; it reflects a real architectural
difference (planv2 front-loads an exhaustive graph compile at construction, v1 apparently does not).

**`plan.Plan` FAILS -- a hard, fast, typed error, not a timeout or exponential blowup -- on every
operation that crosses into a second subgraph, at every S tested including the smallest (S=2):**

```
internal: could not resolve a field: internal: nodesResolvableVisitor: could not select the
datasource to resolve Entity1.id on path query.start.next.id
```

Root-caused and pinned in `scale_v1_test.go` (`TestScaleV1FailsOnPureReferenceStub`,
`-race`-covered): the ring's `next` field needs `Entity_(i+1)`'s output type to resolve in subgraph
i's SDL, so subgraph i declares a bare reference stub (`type Entity_(i+1) @key(fields: "id") { id:
ID! }`) carrying ONLY the key field -- a standard, realistic federation pattern for a subgraph that
references an entity it does not own and contributes zero fields of its own. v1's
`nodesResolvableVisitor` cannot disambiguate which datasource resolves `Entity_(i+1).id` when one
subgraph owns the type fully and the OTHER subgraph's entire declaration for that type is external
with no owned field alongside it. This reproduces identically at S=2 with a two-field, no-filler
operation (`{ start { id next { id } } }`) -- it is **not scale-dependent** and not the already-known
partial-union planning-time deadlock (`differential.TestV1PartialUnionCorroboration`): different code
path (`nodesResolvableVisitor` vs. the union-exclusivity planner), different symptom (a fast typed
error vs. a deadlock), different trigger (a pure reference stub vs. mutually-exclusive union
members). It appears to be a genuinely new v1 failure mode, surfaced by this generator's topology,
worth its own registry entry outside this benchmark's scope.

Because of this, span=1 (the one query shape in this suite that never crosses subgraphs) is the ONLY
point where v1 and planv2 can be compared head-to-head on the ring topology:

**Not a like-for-like comparison** -- v1's number is construction-inclusive (fresh `NewPlanner` +

### 5.6 Operation-scoped mode (FORMAL_SPEC Section 6.5): both-mode tables

The operation-scoped search mode settles, per plan, only the backward closure of the operation's

conformance 138/140 + 2 identical typed refusals, the differential mode-equality pass, the
720-instance dual-mode brute-force oracle); only resource-guard behavior may differ (Section 6.5 of the

unchanged.

## 7. Pathological-shape sweep (M15): deep abstract-type nesting

Section 6 stresses *breadth* (many subgraphs, wide entities). Section 7 stresses the shape class where the
documented planning pathology of type-explosion query planners (the algorithm family v1 descends
from) lives: **deep nesting of abstract (interface) types with multiplicative plan-option growth** --
one downcast branch per interface implementation (M), compounded per nesting level (D), times
per-leaf multi-subgraph resolution alternatives (@shareable overlap R), while the operation *text*
stays linear in D. A type-explosion planner branches M^D ways at the leaves; planv2's
settle-per-node model predicts no such cartesian blow-up. Section 7 measures both claims on the **same**
graph.

### 7.1 The shape family (`patho_gen_test.go`, `patho_export_test.go`)

Parameters: **D** interface types `PIface0..PIface{D-1}`, each with **M** implementations that are
entities (`@key "id"`) owned by **R** subgraphs (`@shareable`-overlapping resolution -- R equal-cost
alternatives per step), out of **S** subgraphs. Every interface declares `common: String` and (below
the last level) `child: PIface{d+1}` -- an interface field *returning the next interface*, so walking
`child` compounds one type-explosion per level. Any subgraph resolving a field that returns
`PIface{d}` carries pure **reference stubs** (`@key(resolvable: false)`, interface fields `@external`)
for the impls it does not own -- the Fed2 idiom for "may reference, must jump to an owner". Three
operation families (all with operation text linear in D, or D+M for leaffan):

- **walk** -- selects only interface fields (`common`, `child`) through all D levels; no downcast.
- **frag** -- two inline fragments per level, each selecting an impl's exclusive `only{d}_{j}` field
  (forcing a downcast + an owner jump), recursing through `child`.
- **leaffan** -- walks interface `child` to the last level, then fans out one fragment per
  implementation (M leaf branches, each an owner jump).

One source of truth: the in-process legs derive `[]plan.DataSource` from the emitted Fed2 SDL via
`audit.BuildDataSources`; the identical SDL also composes cleanly with `@apollo/composition` 2.14.2
(Section 7.4). Sweep grid **M in {5,10,20} x D in {2,3,4,5} x S in {10,20}, R = 2** (the Section 6.2 per-leg methods
apply unchanged, except: three ops instead of a span walk).

### 7.5 Reading Section 7 honestly

- **The `walk` result carries a representation caveat.** planv2 plans `walk` as a **single fetch at
  every size** (M5/D2 through M20/D5), whereas `frag` (3->6 fetches) and `leaffan` (5->17 fetches) scale
  with the shape as expected. A one-fetch `walk` plan was **not** cross-validated as semantically
  complete against a reference executor, and it may partly reflect an **in-process vs. composition
  representation divergence** (the in-process leg derives capability from SDL via
  `audit.BuildDataSources`; a composed-supergraph consumer derives it from `@apollo/composition`
  output, and the two representations can disagree about what a `resolvable:false` interface stub
  permits) rather than pure planner capability. The **`frag` and `leaffan` findings are the robust
  ones**: they force downcasts to owner-exclusive fields, and planv2 produces proportional
  multi-fetch plans (fetch counts and allocations both scale with M and D).
- **Pins & environment.** Section 7.2's grid is from the final quiesced re-run at commit **`b9bad396`**
  (2026-07-17, single quiesced machine, serial runs; the three earlier runs at `782d706b`,
  `f80e07e9` and `1daf64e1` agreed within a few percent of each other on every cell -- this run
  reads ~+5-12% above them, the session's ambient shift, shape unchanged). Section 7.3's v1 cells were
  NOT re-run (v1 did not change) and remain benchstat medians over `-count=3 -benchtime=1x` at
  `f80e07e9`. Section 7.4's composition numbers were NOT re-run (out of the first-party re-run's scope)
  and remain pinned to `@apollo/composition` **2.14.2** at `782d706b`. Go 1.25.5, Apple M4 Pro
  (14 logical CPUs, darwin/arm64). Each Section 7.2 number is a single run (planv2 mean over the
  `-benchtime=1s` iteration count); re-run before citing externally.

## Caveats (read before quoting any number above)

- **Single machine.** As of the `b9bad396` final re-run, Section 1 and Section 4 are benchstat medians over
  `-count=10`, Section 5.2 and Section 5.3's re-earned S=200 cells over `-count=5`, and Section 7.3's v1 cells over
  `-count=3` (at `f80e07e9`) -- but Section 7.2's grid remains one run per cell, Section 5.5 is one-shot by
  design, and everything is still ONE machine, one session, one seed per instance. benchstat needs
  >=6 samples for a confidence interval, so the count=5/count=3 legs report a median without a CI.
  This run also MEASURED a cross-session ambient shift (~+10-20% wall time on unchanged-code
  controls with byte-identical allocations -- see the methodology block), which is exactly the kind
  of drift single-machine numbers are exposed to. Treat every number as a directional snapshot,
  not a certified measurement -- re-run before citing externally.
- **Plan-level only.** Every number in Section 1/Section 2 comes from `plan.Plan` construction (planning), not
  execution -- no subgraph round-trips, no resolver work, no network. Section 3/Section 4 are pure `search` package
  measurements (no `ast`/`plan` involvement at all).
- **Construction-inclusive timing (Section 1).** Each benchmarked iteration is `NewPlanner` + parse/normalize
  + `Plan`, not steady-state per-request `Plan`-only cost. A real gateway builds the planner once per
  supergraph and reuses it across many requests -- `hypergraph.Build`'s one-time compile cost is
  amortized away in that regime but is charged on *every* iteration here for both planners equally.
  Since both arms pay the identical parse/normalize overhead and each planner's own one-time-build
  cost, the *relative* comparison (v1 vs planv2) is fair; the *absolute* per-op numbers are not
  representative of steady-state per-request latency for either planner.
- **v1-unplannable fixtures excluded from the head-to-head.** partial-union has no v1 planning-time
  number at all (a genuine v1 defect, not a planv2 win being hidden) -- reported planv2-only, not
  averaged into any comparison.
- **Small, curated corpus for Section 1/Section 2.** Five differential fixtures plus three synthetic cases is not a
  statistically representative sample of the full 199-case audit corpus's shape distribution -- it is
  the shapes the differential harness happens to cover (entity jumps, `@requires`, multi-hop, fan-out)
  plus three deliberately larger synthetic cases. Section 3 (gap distribution) is the only measurement here
  that runs over the full audit corpus.
- **Fetch-count parity, not proof of a general bound.** Section 2's <= holds on all 4 comparable fixtures but
  none of them exhibit a *strict* reduction -- this snapshot does not demonstrate MERGE's co-location
  pass finding savings v1 misses; it only demonstrates the M1 non-regression bar is met on this corpus.
- **Scaling curve is one instance family.** Section 4's near-linear result is on the `property_test.go`
  generator's shape (sparse DAG, <=3 tails/edge, 3 fixed goals, guaranteed reachability) at a single
  seed per size -- it is evidence *for* near-linear scaling on this shape, not a worst-case bound
  (PREFLIGHT's `L19` structural estimate and the `L7` StateCap backstop exist precisely because
  adversarial shapes can differ).
- **Section 5 is ONE synthetic shape, one seed per size, single machine, one run each (`-benchtime=1x`).**
  The ring+sprinkle topology (a single dominant entity-jump chain, sparse multi-key/composite-
  key/`@requires` sprinkling, no interfaces/unions, no mutations) is a deliberate approximation of
  "200 subgraphs with realistic federation shape", not a sample from a distribution of real
  supergraphs -- real enterprise supergraphs vary enormously in topology (star-shaped vs. deeply
  chained, entity-dense vs. entity-sparse, heavy `@requires` fan-out vs. none) and Section 5's numbers should
  not be read as bounds on any particular real deployment. Section 5's benchmark legs run at
  `-benchtime=1x` (a single iteration per sample) because the largest legs (S=200, ~15MB SDL,
  64k-edge H) are too slow for Section 1-4's `-benchtime=200ms` auto-scaling without looping the expensive
  setup many times over; the `1daf64e1` re-run wraps that in `-count=5` and reports benchstat
  medians (observed spread was small -- e.g. S=200/span=1 ranged 59.4-61.7ms over the 5 samples),
  which is better than the original single-sample numbers but still below the >=6 samples benchstat
  wants for a confidence interval.
- **Section 5.3's p50/p95 are order statistics over a 4-point span sweep, not percentiles of a real
  distribution.** A genuine p50/p95 needs many independent samples at a FIXED span; Section 5.3 has one
  sample each at four different spans. The p50/p95 labels are used because that is the form the M15
  report asked for, but the honest reading is "2nd-cheapest and most-expensive span tested at this
  S", not "the median and 95th-percentile latency a real workload would see".
- **Section 5.3's `obligation.Build` quadratic-in-S finding is a measurement on ONE topology, not a proven
  general complexity bound.** It is reported because it is real, reproducible (three S values, a
  clean ~4x-per-doubling signal), and load-bearing for the "does planning explode at scale" question
  -- but it is not a formal complexity analysis of `obligation.Build`, and a different topology (fewer
  entity types, more fields per type; or vice versa) could shift the constant or the exponent.
- **Section 5.4's v1 divergence is a NEW, narrowly-isolated finding, not a general "v1 can't do multi-hop"
  claim.** `multiHopSubgraphs`/`entity_jump.go` (Section 1's differential-corpus fixtures) ARE v1-plannable
  multi-subgraph entity chains -- the difference isolated in `scale_v1_test.go` is specifically a
  reference subgraph that owns ZERO fields of the referenced type (a pure key-only stub), not
  multi-hop entity resolution in general. Do not extrapolate this into "v1 fails at N-subgraph scale"
  claims beyond what was actually measured.
